# Marshall (1931) vol. II, chapter on seals: observations that bear on the statistics

Read from PDF pages 24-56 of archive.org item in.ernet.dli.2015.107493. These are
Marshall's/Mackay's own statements, quoted in substance, with the finding each bears on.

1. **The same inscription can appear over different animals.** Of the seal whose two faces
   are published as Nos. 252 and 378, the same inscription occurs on both faces above
   animal devices that are entirely different.
   -> Direct support for FINDINGS 2 (sign-motif associations vanish after deduplication).
   The text is not a label for the animal.

2. **Two different seals can carry the same inscription.** The same inscription occurs on
   two potsherds, and it is evident that the same seal was not used for both impressions.
   -> Bears on FINDINGS 13, 39 and the identity question: a text is reproducible across
   objects, so it names something (a person, office or entity) rather than serving as a
   unique per-object identifier.

3. **Two-sided seals can carry DIFFERENT inscriptions on the two faces.** Seal L 323 has a
   unicorn on both faces "but not the same inscription".
   -> The face question (FINDINGS 24, 26) is real and unresolved: an object may carry two
   distinct texts, which no available corpus records.

4. **No true sealings had been found at Mohenjo-daro by 1931.** Marshall notes that not a
   single true sealing - an impression on clay attached to something - had then been found
   there, and that the surviving impressions are on baked clay or faience. He argues
   against the amulet reading on the ground that most seals have a projecting boss at the
   back, which an amulet worn on the person would not need.
   -> Tempers the "seals closed bundles and doors" reading used in earlier sessions: that
   evidence comes from other sites, not from Mohenjo-daro's own material as published here.

5. **A sealing found in Mesopotamia (Umma/Yokha) carries an Indus-type bull and script,
   and its back bears the impression of woven material**, suggesting it was attached to a
   bale that probably came from India.
   -> The clearest published use-case: goods sealed for transport, at the far end of a
   trade route. Relevant to FINDINGS 5 (foreign finds).

6. **Cylinder seals at Mohenjo-daro are not perforated for suspension**, unlike elsewhere;
   only five were found.
