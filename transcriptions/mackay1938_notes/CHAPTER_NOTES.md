# Mackay (1938) vol. I, seals chapter: statements bearing on the statistics

Read from `data/mackay1938_vol1.txt` (archive.org TVA_BOK_0009130). Mackay excavated four
seasons after Marshall, so these statements update Marshall (1931).

1. **Sealings DO exist at Mohenjo-daro, but only about seven** were once affixed to matting
   or rough textile. The rest "were never attached to anything else, nor did they serve to
   mark merchandize, as did sealings of other countries."
   -> Updates Marshall's "not a single true sealing", but only just: bale-sealing existed
   and was rare in the surviving material.

2. **Mackay explains the scarcity as preservation, not practice**: the material impressed
   was "probably ordinary clay, which has not survived the damp and saltiness of the soil",
   and unbaked clay objects are almost entirely absent from the site.
   -> The absence of sealings is taphonomic as much as functional; arguments from their
   absence are weak.

3. **His reading of seal and animal**: where an object bears both an inscription and an
   animal it was "probably used as seals and amulets combined", the animal carrying the
   amuletic value and "only the inscription used for sealing purposes".

4. **Two-line seals: he reads the upper line as the owner's name.** Of a three-headed-animal
   seal: "the lower line of inscription may have been added at the same time... this
   additional line refers to the nature or meaning of the three-headed beast, the owner's
   name being in the usual place above."
   -> Independent, qualitative agreement with FINDINGS 34, which found the second line to
   be a distinct register rather than a continuation.

5. **Reading direction**: of a long inscription, "it would apparently have been written on
   the actual seal from right to left." Consistent with FINDINGS 32c, which found the
   forward/backward asymmetry running that way.

6. **The same inscription recurs with the same animal across copper tablets**, and one
   tablet "bears the same inscription as that on No. 11, though an elaborate compound
   pictograph takes the place of the animal."
   -> Supports the corpus observation that a text can appear on more than one object type
   and with different devices.
