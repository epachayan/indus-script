# Abstract & forum blurb

## Formal abstract (~200 words)

We present two machine-readable catalogues of Mohenjo-daro seals, hand-transcribed from
the excavation reports of Mackay (1938, 709 rows with block/house/room and level) and
Marshall (1931, 560 rows with excavation area and depth), together with text features
coded from all thirteen of Mackay's seal plates (531 seals, 419 joined to find-spots,
inter-pass reliability kappa 0.89), a held-out validated set of 90 distributional
constraints on Indus sign sequences (1.08% violation rate on 2,585 held-out applications),
and a reproducible pipeline of genre- and size-matched structural comparisons against
Sumerian, Egyptian, Linear A/B, proto-cuneiform and Proto-Elamite corpora. Indus sign
sequences carry non-first-order dependencies (7-8% excess conditioning to distance 5, where
a bigram control collapses to 0.4%), condition as tightly as syllables within a Mycenaean
Greek word, and show a text-final slot that is strongly preferred but not closed, replicating
independently at Harappa. Genre-matched Egyptian title-and-name labels place their fixed
element first and variable element last; Indus does the opposite. Text form does not vary
with depth or find-spot across the complete coded corpus, and one earlier positive result
failed to replicate on independent data. Ledger, container-label and astral/calendrical
readings are not supported. No decipherment is offered or implied: the texts are too short,
there is no bilingual, and the language is unknown.

## Forum TL;DR (~100 words)

Two seal catalogues transcribed by hand from the 1931/1938 excavation reports (OCR failed
on both), text features coded from all of Mackay's seal photographs, 90 positional rules
mined on half the corpus and validated on the held-out half, and controlled comparisons
against six other writing systems. The results are mostly negative and are documented as
such: no ledger, no closed name-set, no link between text and find-spot depth, and one of
our own earlier claims failed to replicate independently. What stands: sign sequences carry
dependencies a bigram model can't produce, the text-final slot is preferred-but-open and
replicates at a second city, and a genre-matched Egyptian comparison shows Indus puts its
fixed element LAST where Egyptian labels put it first. Not a decipherment — code, data and
this note are attached, tear it apart.
